# Sources: SINEs

Date: 2026-02-27
Category: SINEs (short interspersed nuclear elements)

## Primary method papers

1. Wenke T, Döbel T, Sörensen TR, et al. 2011.
   Targeted Identification of Short Interspersed Nuclear Element Families Shows Their Widespread Existence and Extreme Heterogeneity in Plant Genomes.
   Plant Cell 23(9):3117–3128.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1105/tpc.111.088682
   PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21908723/
   PMC full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3203444/
   Why used: Original SINE-Finder algorithm description, motif model, parameterization, and reported limitations.

2. Mao H, Wang H. 2017.
   SINE_scan: an efficient tool to discover short interspersed nuclear elements (SINEs) in large-scale genomic datasets.
   Bioinformatics 33(5):743–745.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw718
   PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28062442/
   PMCID landing: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5408816/
   Why used: SINE_Scan integrated feature framework (copy number + structural signals + hallmark transposition evidence) and benchmark claims.

3. Li Y, Jiang N, Sun Y. 2022.
   AnnoSINE: a short interspersed nuclear elements annotation tool for plant genomes.
   Plant Physiology 188(2):955–970.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiab524
   PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34792587/
   PMCID: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8825457/
   Why used: profile-HMM + structural de novo candidate generation with false-positive filtering to improve sensitivity/precision tradeoff in plants.

4. Liao H, Sun Y, Ou S. 2024.
   Accelerating de novo SINE annotation in plant and animal genomes.
   Mobile DNA 15(1):24.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s13100-024-00331-y
   PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39427206/
   PMCID: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11490119/
   Why used: AnnoSINE_v2 extends SINE annotation to animal genomes and improves computational efficiency and reported benchmark performance.
